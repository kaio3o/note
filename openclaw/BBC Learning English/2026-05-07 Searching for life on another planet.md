## English Gist / Main Idea

This episode discusses whether **life**（生命）could exist on another **planet**（行星）, especially on an **exoplanet**（太陽系外行星）called TRAPPIST-1e. Scientists are interested in it because it is a rocky planet similar in size to Earth and sits in a **habitable zone**（宜居帶）, where conditions might allow life. The programme explains how researchers study a planet's **atmosphere**（大氣層）and look for gases that may make life possible. It also teaches useful vocabulary for talking about scientific possibility, evidence and hope.

## Full Structured Content

### 1. Introduction

The presenters begin by asking whether **life beyond Earth**（地球以外的生命）exists. They point out that the idea of aliens in films may be very different from what scientists are actually looking for.

Instead of imagining dramatic science-fiction scenes, researchers study whether distant planets have the right **conditions**（條件）for life. In this episode, the focus is TRAPPIST-1e, a planet about 40 light years away from Earth.

The main question is not whether people can already live there, but whether the planet has signs of being **potentially habitable**（可能適合居住／生命存在的）.

---

### 2. This Week's Question

The quiz question asks:

How long would it take to drive to the Sun at 100 km an hour?

- a) 70 years
- b) 170 years
- c) 1,700 years

The answer given at the end of the programme is:

**b) 170 years**

This question helps listeners feel how huge distances in **space**（太空）are, even within our own solar system.

---

### 3. What Is TRAPPIST-1e?

TRAPPIST-1e is an **exoplanet**（太陽系外行星）, meaning it is a planet outside our solar system. It orbits a star called TRAPPIST-1.

Scientists are especially interested in TRAPPIST-1e because:

- it is a **rocky planet**（岩石行星）, like Earth
- it is similar in **size**（大小）to Earth
- it is located in the star's **habitable zone**（宜居帶）

A **habitable zone** is an area around a star where a planet may have temperatures suitable for liquid water and, possibly, life. It does not guarantee that life exists, but it makes the planet worth studying.

---

### 4. Studying the Atmosphere

The episode explains that scientists are studying the **potential atmosphere**（可能存在的大氣層）around TRAPPIST-1e.

An atmosphere matters because it affects:

- the planet's **temperature**（溫度）
- whether the surface could support **water**（水）
- whether harmful or helpful gases are present
- whether life could survive there

Researchers have **ruled out**（排除）some possibilities. For example, they do not think the atmosphere is mostly hydrogen or carbon dioxide. This is important because too much of these **greenhouse gases**（溫室氣體）could make the planet extremely hot.

Instead, there are small clues that the atmosphere might be **dominated**（由……主導）by nitrogen. This would be exciting because nitrogen is common in Earth's atmosphere and may help keep a planet's temperature more stable.

---

### 5. Why Nitrogen Matters

The programme does not say that scientists have found life. It is much more careful than that. The evidence is still limited, and researchers need more data.

However, a **nitrogen-dominated atmosphere**（以氮氣為主的大氣層）could be a positive sign because it might help create a more suitable environment for life. Scientists would then study other gases and use **models**（模型）to estimate whether the planet is too hot, too cold or within a life-supporting temperature range.

The idea is that each clue brings researchers one step closer to understanding whether TRAPPIST-1e could support life.

---

### 6. Main Conclusion

The episode's main message is that scientists are searching for life by looking for **conditions**（條件）, not by expecting immediate proof of aliens. TRAPPIST-1e is interesting because it is rocky, Earth-sized and located in a potentially habitable zone.

At the same time, the programme stresses scientific caution. Researchers must collect more evidence before making strong claims. For now, TRAPPIST-1e is a planet with **potential**（潛力／可能性）, and scientists have their **fingers crossed**（祈求好運／抱持希望）that future research will reveal more.

## Vocabulary

### **habitable**（適合居住的／宜居的）
**Meaning:** suitable for people, animals or other living things to live in.  
**中文：**適合生命居住或生存的。  
**Example:** Scientists are searching for planets that may be **habitable**.

### **zone**（區域／地帶）
**Meaning:** an area that has a particular feature or purpose.  
**中文：**具有某種特徵或用途的區域。  
**Example:** A planet in the habitable **zone** may have the right temperature for water.

### **potential**（潛在的／可能的；潛力）
**Meaning:** possibly able to develop into something or have a particular quality in the future.  
**中文：**現在還未完全確定，但未來可能具備某種能力或特質。  
**Example:** TRAPPIST-1e has **potential** because it may have conditions suitable for life.

### **rule out**（排除）
**Meaning:** to decide that something is not possible or is not likely to be true.  
**中文：**排除某種可能性；認為某事不成立。  
**Example:** Scientists have **ruled out** the idea that the planet is full of carbon dioxide.

### **dominated**（由……主導／佔主要地位）
**Meaning:** controlled by, or mainly made up of, one thing.  
**中文：**被某事物控制、主導，或主要由某物構成。  
**Example:** The atmosphere may be **dominated** by nitrogen.

### **fingers crossed**（希望順利／祈求好運）
**Meaning:** an expression used to show that you hope something good will happen.  
**中文：**表示希望事情順利、祝好運的說法。  
**Example:** **Fingers crossed**, future research will find stronger evidence.

## Useful Sentences for English Practice

- TRAPPIST-1e is in a **habitable zone**（宜居帶）around its star.
- Researchers have **ruled out**（排除）some possible gases in the planet's atmosphere.
- A **nitrogen-dominated atmosphere**（以氮氣為主的大氣層）could be an important clue.
- The planet has **potential**（潛力／可能性）, but scientists need more evidence.
- **Fingers crossed**（希望順利）, future observations will tell us more.

節目重點不是說已經找到外星生命，而是說科學家正在分析這顆星球的大氣層。研究人員已經排除它的大氣主要由氫氣或二氧化碳組成的可能性，並發現一些線索顯示它可能以氮氣為主。氮氣可能有助於讓星球維持較適合生命的溫度。不過目前證據仍不足，還需要更多觀測與模型分析。整體來說，TRAPPIST-1e 是一顆有潛力、但尚未能下定論的星球。
