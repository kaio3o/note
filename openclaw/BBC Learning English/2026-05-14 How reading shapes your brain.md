## English Gist / Main Idea

This episode explains how learning to **read**（閱讀）changes the human **brain**（大腦）. Although spoken language is **natural**（自然的）for humans, reading is not something the brain is born ready to do. Instead, when people learn to read, the brain reuses areas originally involved in **vision**（視覺）, **sound**（聲音）, **language**（語言）, **attention**（注意力）and **emotion**（情緒）. The episode also explains that different **writing systems**（書寫系統）, such as alphabet-based English and character-based Chinese, can shape the brain in different ways.

## Full Structured Content

### 1. Introduction

The presenters introduce the topic of **reading**（閱讀）and ask listeners to think about what life was like before they learned to read. They point out that reading can feel ordinary once we know how to do it, but **scientific research**（科學研究）shows that it has a powerful effect on the brain.

The episode focuses on two main ideas:

- Reading is not a **natural ability**（自然能力）in the same way that spoken language is.
- Learning to read can change **brain activity**（大腦活動）, **brain structure**（大腦結構）and brain **connections**（連結）.

The presenters also mention that the language or writing system a person reads can influence how the brain **develops**（發展）.

---

### 2. This Week's Question

The quiz question is about the French author **Marcel Proust** and his very long novel, commonly translated into English as *Remembrance of Things Past* or *In Search of Lost Time*.

The question asks how many words the book contains:

- a) 130,000
- b) 1.3 million
- c) 13 million

The answer given at the end of the programme is:

**b) 1.3 million words**

The presenters also note that the book contains extremely long **sentences**（句子）, including one sentence with more than 900 words.

---

### 3. Is Reading Natural?

The episode explains that many people assume reading is **natural**（自然的）because it is connected to **language**（語言）. Spoken language is a human ability that develops naturally when children grow up surrounded by **speech**（口語／言語）.

However, reading is different. Humans are not born with a **dedicated**（專門的）brain system for reading. According to the experts in the programme, the brain has had enough **evolutionary**（演化的）time to develop systems for seeing and producing sounds, but not enough time to develop a special area only for reading.

So, reading has to be learned through **training**（訓練）and **practice**（練習）.

---

### 4. How the Brain Learns to Read

Because there is no single built-in “reading brain”, the brain builds a reading **network**（網絡）by using several existing systems. These include areas involved in:

- **vision**（視覺）
- **sound processing**（聲音處理）
- **language**（語言）
- **attention**（注意力）
- **emotion**（情緒）

This means reading is a **whole-brain process**（全腦參與的過程）, not just a simple skill handled by one small area.

Learning to read can affect:

- how **active**（活躍的）different brain areas are
- how the brain is physically **structured**（被建構／組成）
- how different parts of the brain **connect**（連接）with one another

The episode presents **deep reading**（深度閱讀）as especially important because it can influence not only how people process information, but also how they think and understand themselves and the world.

---

### 5. Different Writing Systems Shape the Brain Differently

The programme then explains that the type of **writing system**（書寫系統）a person reads can also shape the brain.

For example, English uses an **alphabet-based**（以字母為基礎的）writing system. Chinese uses **characters**（文字／字元）or **symbols**（符號）that represent words and ideas. Because Chinese characters require more **visual memory**（視覺記憶）and **visual processing**（視覺處理）, reading Chinese can develop and activate the brain differently from reading English.

The episode gives an example of a **bilingual**（雙語的）man who could read both Chinese and English. After a **stroke**（中風）damaged parts of his brain, he lost some of his ability to read Chinese but could still read English. This shows that different writing systems may rely on partly different brain **networks**（網絡）.

---

### 6. Main Conclusion

The main message is that reading is a **learned skill**（後天學會的技能）that changes the brain. It is not simply a natural extension of speech. When people learn to read, the brain **reorganises**（重新組織）and connects different systems together.

The episode also highlights that reading is not the same for every language. The brain **adapts**（適應）to the demands of each writing system, so reading Chinese characters and reading alphabet-based English may use different patterns of brain activity.

In short: reading changes the brain, and the way it changes depends partly on what and how we read.

## Vocabulary

### **natural**（自然的）
**Meaning:** coming from nature; something you are born with or develop without special training.  
**中文：**自然形成的；不需特別訓練就會發展出的。  
**Example:** Speaking is often considered **natural** because children learn it when surrounded by language.

### **dedicated**（專門的）
**Meaning:** designed and used for one particular purpose.  
**中文：**為某一特定用途而設計或使用的。  
**Example:** Humans do not have a **dedicated** reading area in the brain from birth.

### **co-opt**（借用／徵用）
**Meaning:** to involve or use someone or something for a purpose, sometimes not its original purpose.  
**中文：**把某人或某物納入使用；借用原本不是為此設計的東西。  
**Example:** The brain **co-opts** areas used for vision and sound to help with reading.

### **circuit**（迴路／連結系統）
**Meaning:** a system of connections.  
**中文：**由多個部分連接而成的系統。  
**Example:** Reading depends on **circuits** connecting many parts of the brain.

### **inevitably**（不可避免地）
**Meaning:** in a way that cannot be avoided or stopped.  
**中文：**無法避免地；必然地。  
**Example:** A writing system with complex symbols **inevitably** requires strong visual processing.

### **intricate**（複雜精細的）
**Meaning:** very detailed and complex.  
**中文：**細節很多、結構複雜的。  
**Example:** Chinese characters can be visually **intricate**.

這集 BBC Learning English 的主題是「閱讀如何塑造大腦」。節目指出，說話是人類較自然發展出的能力，但閱讀不是天生就會的技能。人類的大腦沒有一個專門為閱讀而生的區域，所以在學習閱讀時，大腦會重新利用視覺、聽覺、語言、注意力和情緒相關的區域，建立出閱讀網絡。

節目也說明，不同語言的書寫系統會以不同方式影響大腦。例如英文是字母系統，而中文是字／符號系統，因此中文閱讀需要更多視覺記憶與視覺處理。這表示閱讀不只是理解文字，也會實際改變大腦的連結與運作方式。
